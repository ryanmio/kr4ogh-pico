This is not a KR4OGH flight — it is **VE3VRO**'s pico balloon on 20&nbsp;m
channel&nbsp;343, and as of this snapshot (2026-08-21) it was still up there.
Launched from grid EN94 in Ontario in late June, it has since crossed the
Atlantic, Asia, and the Pacific **almost three times around** — 3,075 decoded
telemetry records spanning 115,000&nbsp;km of track, every one of them pulled
from wspr.live and decoded by this repository's own stack.

It is archived here as a sample for two reasons. First, this flight is the
decoder's proving ground: one full day of it (2026-08-18) was verified
row-by-row against the Traquito Flight Search Dashboard — 67 of 67 records
identical on every field — and frozen as this repo's test vectors. The full
ingest reproduces those 67 records exactly. The comparison is written up in
[`docs/decode-status.md`](https://github.com/ryanmio/kr4ogh-pico/blob/main/docs/decode-status.md).
Second, it is an honest preview of what this site does with a long flight:
altitude steps as the balloon rides different air masses, the solar-only
nightly silences, receiver coverage thinning over the oceans, and a track
that wraps the map edge to edge.

A few honest caveats, because this is someone else's flight observed from
public data: there is no launch point or launch time on record here (the
track simply starts at the first decoded slot), the earliest records catch
the climb-out from near ground level, and no Pass/Fail scoring applies —
those definitions exist for KR4OGH flights with known launches. When F1A and
F1B fly, their archives will sit beside this one with the full story.
